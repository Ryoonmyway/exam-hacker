# Select, adapt, or generate a task

Choose by purpose, not by a blanket “always use originals” or “always generate” rule.

| Need | Preferred task |
|---|---|
| Representative exam wording and structure | A verified, suitable original problem |
| Check transfer after a visible example | An adapted problem with a meaningful changed condition |
| Locate one gap or verify a repair | A focused short task |
| Course theory exists but no exercises | A generated problem grounded in that theory |
| No course evidence | Ask the minimum topic/level boundary, or provide clearly labeled general practice |

Do not require a user-supplied question when a suitable task can be built. Distinguish course alignment from mathematical validity: a valid general exercise is not evidence of exam frequency.

## Before presentation

Identify the target capability and expected endpoint. Check:
- givens and diagrams are sufficient, units/signs/boundaries explicit;
- the answer exists and is determinate to the degree the question claims;
- the task measures the intended skill without excessive unrelated work;
- difficulty and effort fit the known stage and remaining capacity;
- the learner has not already seen the answer under equivalent conditions.

For an original: verify the statement and answer/rubric when available.
For an adaptation: name its source and changed conditions; recompute the solution. Changing numbers is not automatically a transfer test.
For a generated task: derive its solution privately and use meaningful independent checks suited to the endpoint (e.g. substitution and equilibrium, or an independent numerical/analytic method). Two restatements of the same algebra are not independent checks.

Record verification status and a concise receipt without leaking the answer. If correctness cannot be established, simplify or replace the task; an unverified task cannot support definite grading. If the learner still requests an uncertain exploratory task, label its limitation and do not assign a definitive mastery result.

Do not fabricate an official rubric. Without one, use a transparent correctness/pass criterion rather than imaginary exam process marks.

## What to save

In the practice file: stable ID, objective, origin, source locator or general-theory basis, changed conditions if adapted, verification status, exact prompt, estimate, pass condition, and exposure state.

Keep privately derived solutions out of learner-facing records until the attempt or explicit answer request. On later grading, reconstruct/check from the saved statement and sources rather than rely on forgotten chat context.

## Scope

Default to the next useful task, not a full-course bank. If the user explicitly requests a small set or mock paper, agree/derive scope and available time, verify every item, and keep its answers separate from the presented paper. Do not expand an ordinary “continue” into bank generation.
